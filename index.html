The technology is the easy part. Delivery is the hard part.

I lead complex implementations in regulated environments. The technology is rarely what sinks them — they fail on whether the client trusts you enough to tell you what is actually wrong, and whether the team is strong enough to build it. That is the half I am good at, and I can do the technology too.

Adelaide, South Australia · LinkedIn · GitHub

The key to performance is not process. It is people, and treating people like people.

The client half

I build enough rapport to find out what a client actually needs, which is usually not what the brief says. Then I am honest with them — including steering them away from decisions that would hurt them, and decisions that would have paid me. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation, as a working habit rather than a poster on a wall.

When a Commonwealth case-management engagement was heading for cancellation, the problem was not technical. I flew to the client site, worked directly with the user champion, rebuilt the requirements from how the work actually gets done, and came back with a signed-off backlog that the CTO, CEO and client PM all endorsed. Somebody has to go and build the relationship that gets you the truth. That is delivery.

I hold agile principles firmly and agile ceremonies loosely. Pragmatism beats orthodoxy; the point is working software and people who trust each other, not the ritual.

The team half

I grow the people I work with, deliberately — their skills, and their confidence as engineers and as people. A strong team is made of strong people, and strong teams deliver. I would rather lead by encouragement than by pressure, and I have never seen pressure produce anything that lasted.

And the technology really is the easy part

Airlock is a governed multi-agent system: a written constitution, a human-review gate on outward actions, spend controls under a ledger, and an audit trail — built and operated, not demoed.

Beyond Airlock: america-law-graph turns US statutory law into a queryable graph — over 1M statutory sections across all 50 states and the federal system, an MCP server exposing 12 tools for agent-native access, pgvector HNSW retrieval, and a LoRA-distilled Mistral classifier at 85% agreement with its teacher. Solo-built, with an Australian edition in progress. blast-radius-hook is a Claude Code hook that stops an agent ending its turn if it left callers without test coverage — built because I kept catching that mistake by hand.
